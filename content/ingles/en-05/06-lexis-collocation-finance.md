# Lexis II: collocation networks, word families, false friends, precision verbs and the language of finance

This lesson is about precision. At C1 you are expected to know not only what a word means but which words it travels with, which forms it takes, which Portuguese look-alikes will betray you, and which exact verb a careful writer would choose. The final section focuses on the register of finance and accounting, which you already command in Portuguese and now need to control in English at a professional level.

> **Glossário de apoio:** *collocation* = combinação habitual de palavras; *word family* = família de palavras (mesma raiz, classes diferentes); *false friend* = falso cognato; *precision verb* = verbo de sentido exato que substitui um genérico (*say* → *contend*).

## 1. Collocation networks

Rather than learning words in isolation, map them with their partners. Take the noun **risk**:

- Verb + risk: *assess, mitigate, run, pose, carry, incur, offset, spread, hedge against, expose someone to* a risk.
- Adjective + risk: *inherent, material, systemic, negligible, calculated, undue, reputational* risk.
- Risk + noun: *risk appetite, risk assessment, risk exposure, risk management, risk-averse*.

{{en:The committee concluded that the proposed acquisition posed a material risk to the group's liquidity.}}

Now **decision**: *reach / arrive at / take / make / defer / overturn / rubber-stamp* a decision; *a unanimous / hasty / informed / landmark* decision.

And **evidence**: *compelling / overwhelming / anecdotal / circumstantial / conclusive* evidence; *evidence suggests / points to / emerges*.

Key verb-noun collocations frequently tested in CAE: *draw a conclusion, reach a compromise, meet a deadline, raise an objection, set a precedent, lodge a complaint, bear the cost, incur a loss, strike a balance, pursue a strategy, honor a commitment, breach a contract, waive a fee, levy a tax*.

{{en:The firm agreed to waive its fee in order to preserve the relationship.}}

Intensifying adverb + adjective pairs: *bitterly disappointed, highly profitable, deeply concerned, utterly unacceptable, painfully slow, fully aware, grossly overstated, acutely conscious, hopelessly optimistic*.

## 2. Word families

CAE Part 3 (Word Formation) requires you to produce the right form, including negative prefixes and less obvious derivations.

| Root | Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| comply | compliance | comply | compliant / non-compliant | — |
| rely | reliance / reliability | rely | reliable / unreliable | reliably |
| prove | proof | prove / disprove | proven / unproven | — |
| deceive | deceit / deception | deceive | deceptive / deceitful | deceptively |
| vary | variety / variation / variable | vary | various / variable / invariable | invariably |
| assure | assurance | assure | assured | assuredly |
| negligence | negligence | neglect | negligent / negligible | negligently |
| compete | competition / competitor / competitiveness | compete | competitive | competitively |
| profit | profit / profitability | profit | profitable / unprofitable | profitably |
| legislate | legislation / legislature | legislate | legislative | — |

Note the trap: *negligent* (careless) vs *negligible* (tiny). {{en:The error was negligible, but the failure to report it was negligent.}}

Common suffix pitfalls: *economic* (of the economy) vs *economical* (saving money); *historic* (important in history) vs *historical* (relating to history); *classic* vs *classical*; *industrial* vs *industrious* (hard-working); *sensible* (reasonable) vs *sensitive* (easily affected).

## 4. Precision verbs

Generic verbs (*say, think, show, make*) are acceptable but weak. Precision verbs tell the reader how something was said or what effect it had.

**Reporting verbs (what the speaker did):**

| Verb | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| assert | state firmly | {{en:The CFO asserted that the provisions were adequate.}} |
| contend | argue, in a dispute | *Critics contend that the model understates risk.* |
| concede | admit reluctantly | {{en:The minister conceded that the forecast had been optimistic.}} |
| maintain | continue to claim | *She maintains that the figures were correct.* |
| acknowledge | accept as true | *The report acknowledges several limitations.* |
| allege | claim without proof | *The regulator alleges breaches of reporting rules.* |
| imply / infer | suggest indirectly / deduce | *The letter implies negligence; we infer that legal action may follow.* |
| stipulate | state as a condition | *The contract stipulates monthly reporting.* |
| refute / rebut | prove wrong / argue against | *The auditors refuted the claim with documentary evidence.* |
| dismiss | reject as unimportant | *The board dismissed the concerns as premature.* |

**Evaluating verbs (what the evidence does):**

| Verb | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| undermine | weaken | {{en:The restatement undermined investor confidence.}} |
| bolster | strengthen | *Strong cash reserves bolstered the firm's position.* |
| corroborate | confirm with evidence | *Bank statements corroborate the client's account.* |
| exacerbate | make worse | *Rate rises exacerbated the liquidity problem.* |
| alleviate / mitigate | make less severe | *The grant alleviated short-term pressure.* |
| hinder / impede | obstruct | *Bureaucracy impedes small-business growth.* |
| underpin | form the basis of | *Three assumptions underpin the forecast.* |
| distort | misrepresent | *One-off items distort the comparison.* |
| validate | prove correct | *Subsequent events validated the decision.* |
| warrant | justify | *The discrepancy warrants further investigation.* |

Verbs of change for reports: *rise, climb, surge, soar, rocket* (up); *fall, decline, dip, slump, plummet* (down); *fluctuate, level off, plateau, stabilize, bottom out, recover, rebound*. Nouns: *a sharp rise, a modest decline, a steady increase, a marked improvement, a slight dip*.

## 6. Typical errors made by Portuguese speakers

| Erro | Correção | Comentário |
|---|---|---|
| ~~to attend a client~~ | to serve / assist / deal with a client | *Attend* = comparecer a (*attend a meeting*); "atender" é *serve, assist, deal with*. |
| ~~The company had a prejudice of 2 million.~~ | The company incurred a loss of 2 million. | *Prejudice* = preconceito. |
| ~~Eventually we have delays.~~ | Occasionally we have delays. | *Eventually* = por fim. |
| ~~The result was negligent.~~ | The result was negligible. | Família de palavras. |
| ~~economical crisis~~ | economic crisis | *Economic* = da economia. |
| ~~actives and passives~~ | assets and liabilities | Termos contábeis não são cognatos. |
| ~~The balance sheet was done.~~ | The balance sheet was prepared / drawn up. | Colocação. |
| ~~to realize the audit~~ | to carry out / conduct the audit | *Realize* = perceber. |

## Reading: critical text

Read the extract from a research summary and evaluate the author's choice of verbs. Which claims are strongly asserted, which are hedged, and is the balance appropriate?

> This study examines whether the adoption of international accounting standards bolsters the comparability of financial statements across borders. Drawing on a sample of 412 listed firms, we find compelling evidence that mandatory adoption is associated with a marked reduction in reporting discrepancies. However, the results also indicate that firms with weak internal controls continue to exhibit material inconsistencies, which suggests that standards alone cannot ensure compliance. Several commentators have contended that adoption costs outweigh the benefits for smaller issuers; our data neither corroborate nor refute this claim, as the sample is skewed toward larger entities. We concede that the measurement of "comparability" remains contested. Nevertheless, the findings underpin the case for continued convergence and warrant further investigation into the role of enforcement. Policymakers should resist the temptation to infer that a single framework will, by itself, eliminate distortion.

Questions: Identify three precision verbs and explain what each adds. Find one hedged claim and one unhedged claim. Does the final sentence undermine or support the study's main finding?

## Na prática

Collocation is where an accountant's English is judged. Clients notice when the verb fits the noun: {{en:We recorded a provision for doubtful debts and wrote off the remaining balance.}} Precision verbs sharpen audit findings: {{en:The evidence corroborates management's estimate but does not warrant a full reversal.}} Keep a personal list of common finance collocations and check it before every report you send.

## Resumo

- Learn vocabulary as collocation networks (*pose / mitigate / hedge against a risk; compelling / anecdotal evidence*), not as isolated words.
- Control word families and their traps: *negligent / negligible, economic / economical, historic / historical*.
- C1 false friends include *eventually, comprehensive, assist, resume, prejudice, anticipate, realize, support*.
- Replace generic verbs with precision verbs: reporting (*assert, contend, concede, allege, stipulate*) and evaluating (*undermine, bolster, corroborate, exacerbate, underpin, warrant*).
- Master the finance register: *accruals, depreciation, liabilities, equity, audit opinion, compliance, forecast, provision, write-off, going concern*, plus the verbs of change for describing trends.
