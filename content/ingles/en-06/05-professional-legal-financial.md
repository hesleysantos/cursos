# Professional English at C2: contracts, annual reports, board-level communication and the executive summary

This lesson applies proficiency to your own profession: contractual language (*hereinafter, notwithstanding, indemnify*), the annual report and the auditor's opinion, board-level communication and negotiation, and the executive summary. You already understand the accounting; the task is to command the English.

> **Glossário de apoio:** *indemnify* = indenizar, isentar de responsabilidade; *warranty / representation* = garantia / declaração; *covenant* = compromisso contratual (em dívida: cláusula restritiva); *severability* = divisibilidade (das cláusulas); *force majeure* = força maior; *unqualified opinion* = opinião sem ressalvas; *emphasis of matter* = parágrafo de ênfase; *key audit matters* = principais assuntos de auditoria; *executive summary* = sumário executivo.

## 1. The grammar of contracts

Legal English is a dialect with its own conventions.

**Modal verbs carry legal force.**

| Modal | Legal meaning |
|---|---|
| shall | mandatory obligation: {{en:The Supplier shall deliver the Goods within thirty days.}} |
| shall not | prohibition |
| may | discretion / right: *The Buyer may terminate this Agreement if…* |
| must | requirement or condition (modern plain-English drafting): *The notice must be in writing.* |
| will | future fact or promise (modern plain-English drafting replaces *shall* with *will* or *must*) |

**Defined terms** take capitals and are introduced once: *Alencar Contabilidade Ltda. (hereinafter "the Firm")*; thereafter the capitalized term means exactly what was defined.

**Archaic compounds** (Lesson 4): *hereinafter* (from here on in this document), *hereunder* (under this agreement), *thereof*, *whereas* (in recitals: *WHEREAS the Client wishes to engage…*).

**Doublets and triplets**: *null and void, any and all, by and between, indemnify and hold harmless, cease and desist*: historically redundant synonyms, but expected.

**Key clause vocabulary:**

- *Indemnity*: {{en:The Contractor shall indemnify and hold harmless the Company against any and all claims arising out of the performance of the Services.}}
- *Representations and warranties*: statements of fact the parties guarantee: *The Seller represents and warrants that the accounts give a true and fair view.*
- *Covenants*: promises to do or not do something; in loan agreements, *financial covenants* (*the Borrower shall maintain a debt-to-equity ratio not exceeding 2:1*).
- *Condition precedent*, *force majeure*, *severability* (invalid clause does not void the rest), *entire agreement*, *governing law and jurisdiction*.
- *Notwithstanding the foregoing* = despite what was just said; *subject to* = conditional upon; *without prejudice to* = without affecting other rights; *save as otherwise provided* = except where stated differently; *in lieu of* = instead of; *mutatis mutandis*, *pro rata*, *bona fide*, *inter alia*, *per annum*.

{{en:Subject to clause 7, and without prejudice to any other remedy, the Company may withhold payment until the defect has been remedied.}}

## 2. Reading an annual report

The annual report has a predictable architecture.

1. **Strategic report / MD&A:** chairman's and CEO's statements (watch the euphemisms), business model, KPIs, principal risks, outlook.
2. **Governance:** board composition, committees (audit, remuneration, nomination), remuneration report.
3. **Financial statements:** *statement of financial position*, *income statement*, *cash flow statement*, *changes in equity*, and the *notes*, where the real information lives (policies, judgments and estimates, contingencies, related parties, subsequent events).
4. **Independent auditor's report.**

Vocabulary of the numbers: *adjusted (non-GAAP) profit* (management's measure, excluding "one-offs"; UK: *underlying profit*) vs *reported (GAAP) profit* (UK: *statutory profit*); *comparable* (UK: *like-for-like*) and *organic growth*; *EBITDA*, *free cash flow*, *net debt*, *leverage* (UK: *gearing*), *impairment charge*, *goodwill write-down*, *restatement*. Read critically: compare *adjusted* with *reported*; read the *judgments and estimates* note; check *going concern* and *subsequent events*.

## 3. The auditor's opinion

The audit report follows a fixed structure under ISA 700 (modified opinions are governed by ISA 705): *Opinion*, *Basis for opinion*, *Key audit matters* (listed entities), *Other information*, *Responsibilities of directors*, *Auditor's responsibilities*.

Four types of opinion:

| Opinion | Wording | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Unqualified (clean) | {{en:In our opinion, the financial statements give a true and fair view of the financial position of the Company as at December 31, 2025.}} (ISA wording; US GAAS: *present fairly, in all material respects*) | No material misstatement found. |
| Qualified | *…except for the effects of the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section…* | One specific material problem; the rest is fine. |
| Adverse | *…the financial statements do not give a true and fair view…* | Pervasive misstatement. |
| Disclaimer | *…we do not express an opinion…* | Could not obtain sufficient evidence. |

An **emphasis of matter** paragraph highlights something properly disclosed without qualifying the opinion; *material uncertainty related to going concern* is a separate section. Key phrases: *we have audited, in accordance with, sufficient and appropriate evidence, nothing has come to our attention*. {{en:Nothing has come to our attention that causes us to believe the statements are materially misstated.}} (Note: this is review-level, negative assurance, weaker than an audit opinion.)

## 4. Board-level communication

Boards want **decisions, not data**. The conventions:

- **Lead with the recommendation**, then the rationale, then the detail. {{en:The committee recommends approving the acquisition at a price not exceeding 40 million, for three reasons.}}
- **Quantify everything**: not *significant savings* but *savings of 1.2 million per annum from year two*.
- **Frame risks in terms of likelihood and impact**, with mitigations: *The principal risk is integration delay (likely, moderate impact), mitigated by retaining the target's management for 24 months.*
- **Use board vocabulary**: *to table a motion* (UK: present; US: postpone!), *to minute*, *to second*, *to carry / defeat a motion*, *to abstain*, *quorum*, *to adjourn*, *matters arising*, *AOB*, *to ratify*, *to note* (acknowledge without approving), *fiduciary duty*, *to recuse oneself*.
- **Register**: formal, impersonal, concise; no hedging where a decision is required.

## 5. Negotiation at C2

Beyond the diplomatic language of Module 5, proficient negotiators control the **frame**.

- Setting the agenda: *Before we discuss price, could we agree on scope?*
- Conditional offers: {{en:If you were able to move on the payment terms, we might be able to look again at the volume discount.}}
- Trading, not conceding: *We could consider that, provided that…*
- Testing and reframing: *Help me understand how you arrived at that figure.* / *I'd see this less as a cost and more as an insurance premium.*
- Summarizing and closing: *So, to make sure I've captured this: you're proposing… Shall we put that in writing?*
- Walking away gracefully: *I don't think we'll bridge this gap today. Let's both reflect and reconvene.*

## 6. Writing an executive summary

The executive summary is read by people who will not read the report. It must stand alone.

**Structure (one page, 250–400 words):**

1. **Purpose** (one sentence): *This report assesses the financial viability of opening a second office in Recife.*
2. **Key findings** (three to five bullets or short sentences, quantified).
3. **Recommendation** (explicit, with the decision required): {{en:We recommend that the board approve the Recife office, subject to securing a lease at or below 180 per square meter.}}
4. **Risks and mitigations** (two or three).
5. **Next steps and timeline.**

**Language:** present tense for findings, *should / recommend that … be* for recommendations, nothing that is not in the report, no unshared jargon, no hedging of the recommendation itself.

## 7. Typical errors made by Portuguese speakers

| Erro | Correção | Comentário |
|---|---|---|
| ~~The supplier will deliver~~ (em contrato tradicional) | The Supplier shall deliver | *Shall* = obrigação em registro jurídico tradicional. |
| ~~indemnize~~ | indemnify | Forma do verbo. |
| ~~the responsible of the audit~~ | the person responsible for the audit / the engagement partner | Adjetivo, não substantivo. |
| ~~balance (patrimonial)~~ | balance sheet / statement of financial position | *Balance* sozinho = saldo. |
| ~~the opinion of the auditor was clean~~ (em relatório) | the auditors issued an unqualified opinion | Termo técnico. |
| ~~to table~~ usado para americano = adiar | Use *present / put forward* para evitar ambiguidade | UK/US opostos. |
| Sumário executivo que começa com histórico | Começar com propósito e recomendação | Leitor ocupado. |
| ~~We need that the board approves~~ | We need the board to approve / We recommend that the board approve | Estrutura. |

## Reading: critical text

The following is the opinion section of a fictitious auditor's report. Read it and identify the type of opinion, the technical terms and what the auditor is actually telling investors.

> We have audited the financial statements of Meridian Logistics Inc. for the year ended December 31, 2025. In our opinion, except for the possible effects of the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section of our report, the financial statements give a true and fair view of the financial position of the group and of its profit for the year. Basis for Qualified Opinion: we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the carrying value of inventory held at the group's Rotterdam warehouse, amounting to 14.2 million, because physical access was restricted during the year-end count. Material uncertainty related to going concern: we draw attention to note 2, which indicates that the group's revolving credit facility expires in September 2026 and that renewal has not yet been agreed. These conditions indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the group's ability to continue as a going concern. Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter.

Questions: Why is the opinion qualified rather than adverse or disclaimed? What is the difference between the Rotterdam issue and the credit-facility issue in terms of the auditor's conclusion? What does "Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter" mean? How would you summarize this report for a non-financial director in two sentences?

## Na prática

This lesson is the daily work of an accounting office that serves international clients. Reading a contract, you track *shall*, *may* and *must* to know who owes what; reading an annual report, you compare adjusted with statutory figures and check the going-concern note. Writing for a board, lead with the decision: {{en:We recommend approving the restructuring, which reduces annual tax exposure by an estimated 12 percent.}} In negotiation with an auditor, state the position and the condition in one sentence: {{en:We can accept the adjustment provided that the comparative figures are restated as well.}} Precision here is both linguistic and professional.

## Resumo

- In contracts, *shall* imposes obligation, *may* grants discretion and *must* states requirements or conditions; defined terms are capitalized; *indemnify and hold harmless, notwithstanding, subject to, without prejudice to* carry precise meanings.
- An annual report moves from strategic narrative to governance to financial statements and notes; read *adjusted* against *statutory*, and check judgments, going concern and subsequent events.
- Auditor's opinions are unqualified, qualified (*except for*), adverse or a disclaimer; *emphasis of matter* and *material uncertainty related to going concern* are flags that do not modify the opinion.
- Boards want the recommendation first, quantified findings, risks with mitigations and precise procedural vocabulary (*table, minute, ratify, recuse*).
- An executive summary stands alone: purpose, quantified findings, explicit recommendation, risks, next steps, in 250–400 words with no hedged recommendation.
