# Proofreading and editing: C2-level errors, register consistency and concision

At C2, the errors that remain are errors of attention, not ignorance: a modifier attached to the wrong noun, two sentences glued with a comma, a collective noun that changes number mid-paragraph, a list whose items do not match. Native professionals make them too. This lesson catalogs them, adds register inconsistency and wordiness, and ends with a flawed business report to edit.

> **Glossário de apoio:** *dangling modifier* = modificador sem referente claro; *misplaced modifier* = modificador mal posicionado; *comma splice* = duas orações independentes unidas por vírgula; *run-on sentence* = frase corrida sem pontuação; *parallelism* = paralelismo; *collective noun* = substantivo coletivo; *concision* = concisão; *redundancy* = redundância; *wordiness* = prolixidade.

## 1. Dangling and misplaced modifiers

A modifier must sit next to the word it modifies and, if it is a participle or infinitive clause, share the subject of the main clause.

| Faulty | Problem | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| *Having completed the audit, the report was issued.* | the report did not complete the audit | {{en:Having completed the audit, the team issued the report.}} |
| *To qualify for the discount, payment must be received by Friday.* | payment is not qualifying | *To qualify for the discount, you must pay by Friday.* |
| *The CFO presented the forecast to the board, which was wildly optimistic.* | the board was optimistic? | *The CFO presented to the board a forecast that was wildly optimistic.* |
| *We only audit listed companies.* (intended: no other companies) | *only* modifies *audit* | *We audit only listed companies.* |
| *She almost reconciled every account.* | she did not reconcile any | *She reconciled almost every account.* |
| *Based on the evidence, the auditor's opinion was qualified.* | the opinion is not based; the auditor is | *Based on the evidence, the auditor qualified the opinion.* (or accept *based on* as a preposition in modern usage) |

Position *only, even, just, almost* immediately before the word they limit. {{en:Only the chairman signed the letter.}} / *The chairman signed only the letter.*

## 2. Comma splices and run-ons

Two independent clauses cannot be joined by a comma alone.

- Splice: ~~The results were strong, the board approved the dividend.~~
- Fixes: *The results were strong; the board approved the dividend.* / *The results were strong, so the board approved the dividend.* / *The results were strong. The board approved the dividend.* / *Because the results were strong, the board approved the dividend.*

Conjunctive adverbs (*however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, consequently*) are **not** conjunctions; they need a semicolon or period before them: {{en:Revenue rose; however, margins fell.}} (not ~~Revenue rose, however margins fell~~). Note also that *however* in the middle of a clause is enclosed by commas: *Margins, however, fell.*

Run-on: ~~The audit took six weeks it should have taken three.~~ → *The audit took six weeks; it should have taken three.*

## 3. Agreement

**Collective nouns** (*board, committee, team, staff, government, company, management*): singular in American English and in formal UK writing when the group acts as a unit; plural in UK English when members act individually. Whatever you choose, **stay consistent**: ~~The board has approved the plan and are confident…~~ → *The board has approved the plan and is confident…*

**Tricky subjects:**

- *The number of errors has fallen* (singular) vs *A number of errors have been found* (plural).
- *Neither the CFO nor the auditors were informed* (verb agrees with the nearer subject).
- *Each of the directors is liable.* / *Every account was checked.*
- *None of the invoices was/were paid* (both acceptable; *was* more formal).
- *The data are / is* (plural in academic usage, singular in business); *criteria, phenomena, media* are plurals (*criterion, phenomenon, medium*).
- *Twenty percent of the revenue comes…* / *Twenty percent of the clients come…* (agree with the noun after *of*).
- *Ten million is a large sum.* (amounts as single units take singular)
- Relative clauses: *She is one of the auditors who have raised concerns* (plural: of the auditors who have) vs *She is the only one of the auditors who has raised concerns.*

{{en:Neither the finance team nor the external auditor was aware of the second bank account.}}

## 4. Parallelism

Items in a list, pairs joined by *and / or / but*, comparisons and correlatives (*both… and, either… or, not only… but also*) must share grammatical form.

- ~~The role involves preparing accounts, to liaise with auditors and tax compliance.~~ → *The role involves preparing accounts, liaising with auditors and ensuring tax compliance.*
- ~~We aim not only to cut costs but also improving service.~~ → *We aim not only to cut costs but also to improve service.*
- ~~The new system is faster, more reliable and it costs less.~~ → *The new system is faster, more reliable and cheaper.*
- Bullets: every bullet begins with the same word class.

{{en:The plan is ambitious, affordable and, above all, achievable.}}

## 5. Other C2-level slips

- **Pronoun reference**: *When the auditor met the CFO, he was nervous.* (who?) Rewrite.
- **Tense consistency** in reports: do not drift between present and past.
- **Fewer / less**: *fewer invoices, less revenue*; *number of* (countable) / *amount of* (uncountable).
- **Who / whom**: *the client to whom the letter was sent* (formal).
- **Which / that**: *the accounts, which were late* (all of them) vs *the accounts that were late* (only some).
- **Apostrophes**: *the company's results* (one), *the companies' results* (several), *its* vs *it's*, *the 1990s*.
- **Hyphens**: *a long-term plan* but *in the long term*; *a 20-page report*.
- **Capitalization**: job titles only before a name (*Chief Executive Maria Lopes* but *the chief executive*).

## 6. Register inconsistency

A single informal item in a formal text (or vice versa) is as jarring as a grammar error.

| Inconsistent | Consistent |
|---|---|
| *The committee has concluded that the proposal is a no-brainer.* | *…that the proposal should clearly be approved.* |
| *Hi Board members, Please find attached the aforementioned documentation.* | *Dear Board members, Please find attached the documents referred to above.* |
| *The firm's liquidity position deteriorated, which is a real bummer.* | *…deteriorated, which is a matter of serious concern.* |
| *We'd like to flag that receivables kinda went up.* | *We should point out that receivables increased.* |

Audit a draft for contractions, phrasal verbs, slang, exclamation marks and first-person *I* in impersonal reports, and for stiff archaisms in friendly emails.

## 7. Concision

Professional English values economy. Common offenders and cures:

| Wordy | Concise |
|---|---|
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| due to the fact that | because |
| a large number of | many |
| conduct an investigation into | investigate |
| it is important to note that | (delete) |
| despite the fact that | although |
| prior to the commencement of | before |
| with regard to | about |
| has the ability to | can |
| past history / end result / basic fundamentals | history / result / fundamentals |

{{en:Owing to the fact that the system was unavailable, we were not in a position to process payments.}} → {{en:Because the system was down, we could not process payments.}} (18 words → 10)

Editing procedure: cut empty intensifiers (*very, really*), redundant pairs (*each and every*), empty openers (*It should be noted that*) and nominalizations hiding a strong verb (*carried out an analysis* → *analyzed*). Aim to cut 20% of a first draft without losing a fact.

## Editing task: a flawed business report

Read the paragraph below from a draft internal report. It contains at least twelve problems (grammar, punctuation, register, concision, parallelism). Identify and correct them before checking the model in the exercises.

> Having reviewed the expenses process, several weaknesses was identified. At this point in time, staff is submitting receipts late, this means the accounts can't be closed on time. The finance team, who are responsible for the month-end close, have flagged that a large number of claims under 200 is not checked at all, which is honestly a bit of a mess. In order to fix the situation we recommend introducing an online form, that managers approve claims weekly, and training for all staff. Only the finance director can currently authorise payments over 5,000, which creates a bottleneck when she is traveling. Despite of these issues the process is basically fine, however it needs to be tightened up ASAP.

Look for: a dangling participle; agreement errors (two); comma splices (two); inconsistent number with *staff* and *team*; an unclear *which*; a non-parallel list; informal items (three); *despite of*; wordy expressions (two); mixed spelling variety (*authorise* is British, *traveling* is American; the course standard is American: *authorize*).

## Na prática

Every document an accounting office sends is proofread for figures and then for language. The slips in this lesson are those that survive native-speaker drafts: a dangling modifier that makes the auditor seem to be the error, a comma splice in a cover letter, a list of recommendations whose items do not match. Build a final pass with three questions: who performs each opening participle, is each comma joining two full sentences, and do the bullets start with the same word class. Then cut: {{en:Owing to the fact that the invoices were not received until May, we conducted a review.}} becomes {{en:Because the invoices arrived in May, we reviewed them.}}

## Resumo

- Modifiers (participles, infinitives, *only / almost*) must attach to the word they describe; check every sentence that opens with an -ing or -ed clause.
- A comma cannot join two independent clauses; use a semicolon, a period or a conjunction, and remember that *however / therefore* are adverbs, not conjunctions.
- Collective nouns take singular or plural consistently; agree with the nearer subject after *neither… nor*, and with the noun after *of* in percentages; *the number of* is singular, *a number of* plural.
- Lists, pairs and correlatives (*not only… but also*) demand matching grammatical forms; bullets start with the same word class.
- Audit drafts for register inconsistency (slang in formal text, archaisms in casual text) and cut wordiness ruthlessly: *due to the fact that → because*, *conduct an investigation → investigate*.
