# Exam and real-world mastery: CPE and IELTS 8.5+ techniques

This lesson is about performing at the top of the scale under time pressure: the demands of CPE Use of English (key word transformation, gapped text, word formation, open cloze), timed writing at C2, and speaking at length on abstract topics as CPE and IELTS Speaking Part 3 require. The same techniques transfer to a board presentation with hostile questions or a report due in two hours.

> **Glossário de apoio:** *key word transformation* = reescrita com palavra-chave; *gapped text* = texto com parágrafos removidos; *word formation* = formação de palavras; *open cloze* = texto com lacunas sem opções; *long turn* = fala longa individual; *to extend an answer* = desenvolver uma resposta; *fluency* = fluência; *coherence* = coerência.

## 1. Key word transformation (CPE Part 4)

Rewrite a sentence using a given word, in three to eight words, keeping the meaning. Each item tests a grammatical transformation plus a lexical one; the answer must be exact.

| Original | Key word | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| I'm sure he didn't mean to offend you. | **INTENTION** | He had no **intention of offending** you. |
| They only realized the error when the auditors arrived. | **UNTIL** | Not **until the auditors arrived did they realize** the error. |
| The company managed to survive despite the recession. | **PULL** | The company managed to **pull through in spite of** the recession. |
| She regrets not having accepted the offer. | **WISHES** | She **wishes she had accepted** the offer. |
| It is likely that the board will reject the plan. | **WELL** | The board **may well reject** the plan. |
| Nobody expected the merger to collapse so quickly. | **CAME** | The collapse of the merger **came as a surprise to** everybody. |
| The report was too technical for most readers. | **SUCH** | It was **such a technical report that** most readers could not follow it. |
| I'd prefer you not to mention this to the client. | **RATHER** | I **would rather you didn't mention** this to the client. |

**Method:** identify the structure the key word forces (*UNTIL* → inversion; *RATHER* → unreal past); identify the lexical change (*survive → pull through*); count words (contractions count as two); check that every element of meaning, including negatives and time references, survives.

{{en:Not until the auditors arrived did they realize the error.}}

## 2. Open cloze (CPE Part 2) and word formation (Part 3)

**Open cloze** tests grammar words: articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, relative pronouns, linkers, parts of fixed phrases (*on_ behalf of, by_ no means, at_ stake*). Read the whole text first, decide the word class and check agreement both ways. The commonest C2 answers are small words carrying large structures: *were* (inversion), *had* (inverted conditional), *whose*, *whereby*, *albeit*, *lest*, *nor*, *such*, *so*, *what*, *far* (*by far, far from*), *little* (*little did*), *should* (*should you*).

**Word formation** at C2 requires compound suffixes and prefixes: *un-, in-, dis-, mis-, over-, under-, out-, counter-, non-*; *-ity, -ness, -ship, -hood, -dom, -ism, -ise, -en, -ify, -some, -worthy, -proof, -laden, -ridden, -free*. Expect two changes at once: *rely → unreliability*; *predict → unpredictably*; *trust → trustworthiness*; *note → noteworthy*; *debt → indebtedness*; *afford → unaffordable*; *mistake → unmistakably*; *regulate → deregulation*; *competent → incompetence*. {{en:The forecasts proved unreliable, and their unpredictability undermined the budget.}}

Check the required **word class** from the gap's position and whether the sense demands a **negative prefix**.

## 3. Gapped text (CPE Part 6) and multiple matching

Paragraphs are removed from a long text; you reinsert them. This tests cohesion (Module 5, Lesson 7): referencing, lexical chains, time sequence and contrast markers. Read for the gist; for each gap, look in the candidates for the **link words at the start** (*But this…, Such…, Nor…, Even so…*) and the **referents** they need; verify both the entry and the exit link. The leftover paragraph is a plausible distractor with no exact referent.

**Multiple matching** (Part 7): scan for **paraphrase**, never for the same words; the question says *reluctance to accept change*, the text says *dug their heels in*.

## 4. Timed writing at C2

CPE Writing Part 1 is a compulsory essay (240–280 words) summarizing and evaluating two short texts; Part 2 offers an article, letter, report or review (280–320 words). IELTS Task 2 is 250+ words in 40 minutes.

**Time plan for 45 minutes:** 5 min analysis and plan; 32 min writing; 8 min editing (Lesson 7 checklist).

**For the CPE Part 1 essay:** identify two key points in **each** text, summarize them in your own words, evaluate them and reach your own position. Never copy phrases; paraphrase (Lesson 6) is directly assessed.

**What takes a script from C1 to C2:** a thesis with a nuance (*The case for regulation is strong, but the texts underestimate the cost of compliance for small firms*); one or two idioms used exactly, not a string; controlled rhetoric (one tricolon, one antithesis, at most one rhetorical question); natural syntactic range (inversion, cleft, participle opening, semicolon, dash); zero register slips; economy.

{{en:What the texts share, and what both overlook, is the assumption that compliance is costless.}}

## 5. Speaking at length on abstract topics

CPE Speaking Part 3 asks for two minutes on an abstract prompt (*Is ambition always a positive quality?*); IELTS Part 3 asks abstract follow-ups. The challenge is **sustained structure**.

**The two-minute architecture:**

1. **Reframe** the question (10 s): *That depends very much on what we mean by ambition.*
2. **Position** (10 s): *On the whole I'd argue it is, with one important caveat.*
3. **Two developed points** (40 s each): claim, reason, example, consequence. Use *for instance, which means that, the upshot being*.
4. **Counterpoint and reconciliation** (20 s): *Admittedly… but on balance…*
5. **Close** (10 s): return to the opening frame with a twist.

**Fluency tools:** time-buying fillers (*That's an interesting way of putting it; I suppose the honest answer is…*); self-correction (*or rather, to put it more precisely*); extension frames (*which in turn, the flip side of that being*); speculation (*were that to happen, one could imagine a situation in which*).

**Register:** educated spoken English, not prose read aloud: contractions normal, fragments acceptable, idioms welcome if exact.

{{en:I suppose the honest answer is that ambition is a double-edged sword: indispensable in moderation, corrosive in excess.}}

## 6. Maintaining fluency under pressure

Fluency breaks down when you search for a specific word, monitor grammar too closely or are interrupted. Paraphrase rather than pause; accept small errors and keep going (hesitation costs more than one wrong article); hold your turn politely (*If I could just finish the point…*) and then yield (*But I'd be interested to hear your view*). Practice two minutes on a random abstract noun daily.

## 7. Real-world transfer

Every exam skill has a professional twin: key word transformation is rewording a contract clause; gapped text is restructuring a colleague's report; word formation is choosing *indebtedness* over *debt* in a credit memo; the Part 1 essay is a balanced briefing from two conflicting consultants; the long turn is answering "What's your view on the acquisition?" at a board dinner.

## Reading: critical text

Below is a model CPE Part 1 essay opening and first body paragraph, responding to two texts on whether companies should be required to publish pay ratios. Analyze what makes it C2.

> Both texts address the growing pressure on companies to disclose the ratio between executive and median employee pay, yet they arrive at opposite conclusions. The first contends that transparency alone will restrain excess, since no board, it argues, wishes to defend a ratio of 300 to 1 in public. The second counters that disclosure has, where introduced, produced embarrassment rather than restraint, and that the real driver of executive pay is a dysfunctional market for talent, which no spreadsheet will fix. There is force in both positions; what neither acknowledges is that transparency and market reform are not alternatives but sequential steps, the first creating the political conditions for the second. Were ratios to be published universally, the resulting debate would, I suspect, prove far more consequential than either writer expects: not because boards are easily shamed, but because shareholders, once equipped with comparable data, rarely remain passive for long.

Questions: Identify the paraphrase of each text's main point. Where is the writer's own thesis, and what structure introduces it? Find the inverted conditional, the cleft and the antithesis. Which hedges prevent the thesis from sounding arrogant?

## Na prática

Exam technique transfers directly to the pressure points of an accounting office: a report due in two hours, a board that asks hostile questions, a regulator's letter that must be answered in exact words. Key word transformation is the skill of restating a client's claim precisely without changing its meaning; gapped-text logic is how you read a long contract for cohesion. Speaking at length is the ten-minute explanation of a tax position: {{en:Let me reframe the question: the issue is not whether we pay, but when the liability crystallizes.}} Under pressure, paraphrase rather than pause: {{en:In other words, the timing drives the cost.}}

## Resumo

- Key word transformations test one grammatical and one lexical change; identify the structure the key word forces, count words exactly (contractions = two) and preserve every element of meaning.
- Open cloze targets small words carrying big structures (*were, had, whose, whereby, nor, such, little*); word formation at C2 requires double changes (*unreliability, trustworthiness, indebtedness*) and the right word class.
- Gapped texts are solved through cohesion: match entry and exit links, referents and lexical chains; multiple matching is solved by paraphrase, never by matching words.
- C2 timed writing has a nuanced thesis, controlled rhetoric, natural syntactic range, zero register slips and economy; paraphrase, never copy, in the Part 1 essay.
- Speak at length using a two-minute architecture (reframe, position, two points, counterpoint, close), buy time with educated fillers, paraphrase rather than pause, and generalize beyond personal anecdote.
